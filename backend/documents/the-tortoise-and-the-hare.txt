The Tortoise and the Hare

A Hare was forever boasting about how fast he could run. "Nobody in the whole forest is as quick as I am," he said to anyone who would listen. "I have never been beaten in a race, and I never will be."

One morning the Tortoise, who had heard this boast many times, looked up from the grass he was chewing and said quietly, "I will race you."

The Hare laughed so hard that he fell over. "You? Race me? You take an hour to cross the path to the pond. Very well, Tortoise, let us race, and let everyone watch."

The animals of the forest agreed on a course. It would start at the old oak tree, run along the stream, climb the hill past the fox's den, and finish at the great stone at the top. The Fox was chosen to be the judge, because the fox was clever and fair, and nobody dared to argue with the fox.

At the signal the Hare shot away like an arrow. In a few moments he was out of sight. The Tortoise set off at his usual pace: one step, then another step, then another step. He did not hurry and he did not stop.

Halfway up the hill the Hare looked back. There was no sign of the Tortoise anywhere. "This is hardly a race at all," he said to himself. "The sun is warm and the grass is soft. I will lie down here for a little while. When I wake up I will still reach the stone long before that slow old Tortoise."

So the Hare lay down under a bush and closed his eyes, and soon he was fast asleep.

Meanwhile the Tortoise kept going. One step, then another step, then another step. He passed the stream. He passed the fox's den. He passed the bush where the Hare lay sleeping, and he did not even stop to look. Slowly, slowly, he climbed towards the top of the hill.

The sun moved across the sky. The shadows grew long. At last the Hare woke with a start. He jumped to his feet and ran up the hill as fast as he had ever run in his life. But when he reached the top, he saw the Tortoise already resting beside the great stone, and the Fox standing next to him.

"The Tortoise is the winner," said the Fox.

The animals cheered. The Hare hung his head, and for once he had nothing to say.

The Tortoise smiled. "Being fast is a fine thing," he said. "But slow and steady wins the race."

Moral: Slow and steady wins the race. A steady effort that never stops can beat great talent that rests too soon.
